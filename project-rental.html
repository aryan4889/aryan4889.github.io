📊 Key Findings & Business Recommendations
South Dublin commuter belt (Blackrock, Dún Laoghaire) shows the strongest YoY rent growth at 12–16% — driven by remote-work demand and limited new supply. This represents the highest investment signal in the dataset.
Cork City rent remains 18% below Dublin for comparable properties, but growth rate (7–8% YoY) suggests convergence — potential early-mover opportunity for property investors seeking value.
3-bedroom properties show the highest per-unit rent inflation (+12% avg across all cities), while studio/1-bed growth has moderated — suggesting family housing supply is most constrained.
Galway City has lowest absolute rents but highest proportion of student-tenant listings (42%), suggesting a different risk profile for buy-to-let investors vs Dublin commuter market.

Sample SQL Query
Aggregating average rent by city and bedroom count with YoY comparison
-- Average rent by city and bedroom type with year-on-year change WITH current_year AS ( SELECT city, bedrooms, AVG(monthly_rent) AS avg_rent_2024, COUNT(*) AS listing_count FROM rental_listings WHERE scraped_year = 2024 GROUP BY city, bedrooms ), prior_year AS ( SELECT city, bedrooms, AVG(monthly_rent) AS avg_rent_2023 FROM rental_listings WHERE scraped_year = 2023 GROUP BY city, bedrooms ) SELECT c.city, c.bedrooms, ROUND(c.avg_rent_2024, 0) AS avg_rent_2024, ROUND(p.avg_rent_2023, 0) AS avg_rent_2023, ROUND((c.avg_rent_2024 - p.avg_rent_2023) / p.avg_rent_2023 * 100, 1) AS yoy_change_pct, c.listing_count FROM current_year c JOIN prior_year p ON c.city = p.city AND c.bedrooms = p.bedrooms ORDER BY yoy_change_pct DESC;
