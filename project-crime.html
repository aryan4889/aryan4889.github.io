🔍 Key Findings for Insurance Analytics
Fraud and cybercrime is the standout risk: a 197% increase over 10 years, accelerating sharply after 2019. Insurance teams should revisit cyber liability and financial fraud policy pricing — traditional actuarial models underestimate this trajectory.
Traditional theft offences (theft from person, vehicle crime, burglary) have all declined significantly post-COVID — likely due to behavioural changes, CCTV expansion and garda resource reallocation. Home contents insurance risk profiles may be improving.
Dublin remains the highest crime rate county at 42.3 offences per 1,000 population, but Limerick shows the sharpest per-capita rise in 2024 (+18% YoY) — a regional signal worth flagging for local risk assessors.
Drug offences are stable-high at ~22,000 per year — correlating with property crime in certain postal districts. A county-level cross-tab of drug and burglary data reveals 3 high-correlation clusters in Dublin, Limerick and Cork.
Project Methodology
From raw CSO open data to actionable insurance insight
01
Data Source
CSO.ie open data portal — Garda Recorded Crime Statistics, 2014–2024. Downloaded as structured CSV.
02
Cleaning (Python)
pandas used to reshape wide-format CSO tables, handle missing county data and standardise category names.
03
Analysis (SQL)
SQLite queries for YoY change, county rankings, per-capita normalisation using CSO population estimates.
04
Excel Report
Dynamic Excel model with slicers by county, category and year. 2-page executive summary for non-technical audience.
Sample SQL Query
Calculating per-capita crime rate and ranking counties
-- Per-capita offence rate by county, ranked for 2024 WITH county_totals AS ( SELECT county, offence_category, SUM(offence_count) AS total_offences, MAX(population_2024) AS population FROM garda_crime WHERE year = 2024 GROUP BY county, offence_category ), ranked AS ( SELECT county, SUM(total_offences) AS all_offences, population, ROUND(SUM(total_offences) * 1000.0 / population, 1) AS rate_per_1000, RANK() OVER (ORDER BY SUM(total_offences) * 1000.0 / population DESC) AS crime_rank FROM county_totals GROUP BY county, population ) SELECT crime_rank, county, all_offences, rate_per_1000 FROM ranked WHERE crime_rank <= 10 ORDER BY crime_rank;
