💡 Key Business Findings
Electronics drives the most revenue (38% of total) but has the highest return rate (14.1%) — meaning true net revenue is closer to 33% of total. Without adjusting for returns, marketing decisions using gross figures would overprice the category's contribution.
The top 20% of customers by LTV (€910+ avg) account for 61% of all revenue but represent only 8% of the customer base. This segment should be the focus of loyalty programme investment and retention campaigns.
Month-on-month revenue grew 18%, but repeat purchase rate improvement (34% → 41%) is the more strategically significant signal — it suggests product satisfaction is improving and CAC is being spread across more orders per customer.
Sportswear and Health categories show the lowest return rates (under 4%) and consistent MoM growth — making them the most margin-efficient categories in the portfolio. Recommended for increased stock allocation and ad spend.

Sample SQL Query
Customer lifetime value calculation with segment ranking
-- Customer Lifetime Value with segment labelling WITH customer_revenue AS ( SELECT c.customer_id, c.name, c.signup_date, COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id) AS total_orders, SUM(o.total_value) AS gross_ltv, SUM(COALESCE(r.refund_value, 0)) AS total_returns, SUM(o.total_value) - SUM(COALESCE(r.refund_value,0)) AS net_ltv FROM customers c LEFT JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id LEFT JOIN returns r ON o.order_id = r.order_id GROUP BY c.customer_id, c.name, c.signup_date ) SELECT customer_id, name, total_orders, ROUND(net_ltv, 2) AS net_ltv, CASE WHEN net_ltv >= 910 THEN 'VIP' WHEN net_ltv >= 400 THEN 'High Value' WHEN net_ltv >= 150 THEN 'Mid Value' ELSE 'Low Value' END AS ltv_segment, NTILE(5) OVER (ORDER BY net_ltv DESC) AS quintile FROM customer_revenue ORDER BY net_ltv DESC;
